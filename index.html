Verbal Intelligence

Verbal Intelligence tests logical reasoning expressed through words and numbers. It checks how quickly and accurately you can spot relationships, patterns, and rules — not how much you've memorised.

  • Analogies: Identify the exact relationship inside the first pair (part-to-whole, worker-to-tool, cause-effect, synonym/antonym) before matching it to the answer choices.
  • Classification (Odd One Out): Group items by category first; the odd item usually breaks one specific shared property, not an obvious one.
  • Number & Letter Series: Check differences, ratios, alternating patterns, or squares/cubes between consecutive terms — don't assume a simple +1/-1 rule.
  • Coding-Decoding: Map each letter to a shift value along the alphabet; double-check the shift direction (forward vs backward).
  • Blood Relations & Direction Sense: Sketch a quick family tree or compass on scratch paper instead of tracking relationships mentally.
  • Syllogisms: Draw simple circle (Venn) diagrams for "All/Some/No" statements before picking a conclusion — don't rely on gut feeling.

Q1. Pen is to Write as Knife is to:
A) Sharp
B) Cut ✓
C) Kitchen
D) Blade
Explanation: A pen's function is to write; a knife's function is to cut. The relationship is 'object to its function', not to a related noun like Sharp/Kitchen/Blade.
Q2. Find the odd one out.
A) Triangle
B) Square
C) Circle
D) Cube ✓
Explanation: Triangle, Square and Circle are all 2-D (flat) shapes. Cube is a 3-D solid, so it doesn't belong with the rest.
Q3. Complete the series: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ?
A) 36
B) 40
C) 42 ✓
D) 44
Explanation: The differences between terms are 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 (increasing by 2 each time). 30 + 12 = 42.
Q4. If BOOK is coded as CPPL, how is WORD coded?
A) XPSE ✓
B) XQSE
C) XPES
D) YPSE
Explanation: Each letter is shifted forward by 1: B→C, O→P, O→P, K→L. Applying the same rule: W→X, O→P, R→S, D→E, giving XPSE.
Q5. Doctor is to Hospital as Teacher is to:
A) Student
B) Book
C) School ✓
D) Classroom
Explanation: A Doctor's primary workplace is a Hospital; a Teacher's primary workplace is a School (Classroom is a room inside the school, not the institution itself).
Q6. Find the next term: A, C, F, J, O, ?
A) S
B) T
C) U ✓
D) V
Explanation: The gap between letters increases by 1 each time: +2, +3, +4, +5, so the next gap is +6. O + 6 letters = U.
Q7. Pointing to a man, a woman said, 'His mother is the only daughter of my mother.' How is the woman related to the man?
A) Mother ✓
B) Sister
C) Aunt
D) Grandmother
Explanation: 'Only daughter of my mother' means the woman herself (since she is her own mother's only daughter). So the man's mother is the woman herself — she is his mother.
Q8. Choose the odd one out.
A) Rose
B) Lily
C) Lotus
D) Mango ✓
Explanation: Rose, Lily and Lotus are all flowers. Mango is a fruit, so it does not belong to the group.
Q9. If North becomes East, East becomes South, what does South become?
A) North
B) West ✓
C) East
D) South-West
Explanation: Each direction is being rotated 90° clockwise (N→E, E→S). Following the same 90° clockwise rotation, South → West.
Q10. Complete the analogy: Bird is to Nest as Bee is to:
A) Honey
B) Sting
C) Hive ✓
D) Flower
Explanation: A Bird lives in a Nest; a Bee lives in a Hive. Honey and Sting are things bees produce/do, not where they live.

Non-Verbal Intelligence

In the real computer-based test, these questions are shown as images (shapes, patterns, and figures), not words. Since you can't practise the exact on-screen images here, this section explains every question type and the reasoning method to apply, so you recognise the pattern instantly during the real test.

  • Series Completion: A sequence of figures changes by rotation, added/removed lines, or shading at each step. Find the ONE consistent rule and extend it.
  • Odd Figure Out: One figure among 4–5 differs in number of sides, symmetry, or an internal pattern.
  • Mirror Image: How a figure looks when reflected across a vertical or horizontal axis — left and right elements swap places.
  • Water Image: Like a mirror image, but reflected across a horizontal axis (as if seen in still water below the figure) — the image is flipped top-to-bottom.
  • Paper Folding & Cutting: Track how a folded, punched paper looks when unfolded — punches mirror across every fold line.
  • Embedded Figures: A simple shape hidden inside a busier diagram — scan systematically left-to-right, top-to-bottom.
  • Cubes & Dice: Given 2–3 views of the same die, use elimination to work out which face is opposite which.

Practice tip: Use any AFNS/ISSB-style non-verbal reasoning app or workbook with actual diagrams, and time yourself to under 20 seconds per question. For mirror/water images, practise with a real pocket mirror against a printed diagram until the flip becomes instantly obvious.

Q1. A figure is reflected across a VERTICAL line drawn through its centre. What happens to its left and right halves?
A) They stay the same
B) They swap places ✓
C) They both disappear
D) They rotate 180°
Explanation: A vertical mirror line swaps left and right — whatever was on the left side of the original appears on the right side of the reflection, and vice versa.
Q2. A 'water image' of a figure is best described as a reflection across which axis?
A) Vertical axis
B) Horizontal axis ✓
C) Diagonal axis
D) No axis — same as original
Explanation: A water image simulates a reflection in water lying below the object, so it flips across a horizontal axis (top becomes bottom).
Q3. In a series of figures, each next figure gains one extra dot in a fixed pattern. What should you check first?
A) Colour of dots
B) Number of dots added at each step ✓
C) Font of any letters
D) Size of the whole figure
Explanation: When a series changes by a repeating count, the number added per step (the numeric rule) is the key pattern to identify before choosing the next figure.
Q4. A square sheet of paper is folded in half and a hole is punched near the fold. When unfolded, how many holes will appear?
A) 1
B) 2 ✓
C) 3
D) 4
Explanation: A single fold doubles the layers of paper at the punch point, so one punch through both layers produces 2 holes when the paper is unfolded, mirrored across the fold line.
Q5. Which technique helps most when solving 'odd figure out' visual questions?
A) Memorising all shapes in advance
B) Comparing number of sides, symmetry, and internal pattern one at a time ✓
C) Picking the largest figure
D) Picking the figure with the most colour
Explanation: Systematically comparing sides, symmetry, and internal details isolates the single property that differs, which is how the odd figure is identified.
Q6. If Face 1 shows a dot and Face 2 (adjacent, from a different view) shows a star on the same die, what technique finds the face opposite the dot?
A) Guessing
B) Elimination using all given rotated views ✓
C) Assuming opposite faces always match
D) Ignoring the other views
Explanation: Cube/dice questions are solved by comparing all given rotated views and eliminating which faces cannot be opposite each other, narrowing down the correct pair by elimination.

English

English MCQs test grammar accuracy, vocabulary range, and comprehension speed.

  • Subject-Verb Agreement: Singular subjects take singular verbs, even when separated from the verb by a phrase (e.g., "The list of items is long").
  • Tenses: Revise all 12 tense forms; pay special attention to Present Perfect vs Simple Past, and tense shifts in reported speech.
  • Parts of Speech: Be able to instantly identify nouns, pronouns, adjectives, verbs, adverbs, prepositions and conjunctions in a sentence.
  • Active & Passive Voice: Practice converting sentences across all tenses.
  • Direct & Indirect Speech: Know the pronoun, tense, and time-word changes required when converting.
  • Articles & Prepositions: The most common trick area — revise fixed preposition phrases (e.g., "married to", "depend on", "consist of").
  • Vocabulary: Build daily lists of synonyms, antonyms, and one-word substitutions.

Q1. Choose the correctly spelled word.
A) Recieve
B) Receive ✓
C) Receeve
D) Receve
Explanation: The rule 'i before e except after c' does not apply here in the usual direction — this word is an exception where e comes before i: R-E-C-E-I-V-E.
Q2. Fill in the blank: She has been living here ___ 2015.
A) for
B) since ✓
C) from
D) at
Explanation: 'Since' is used with a specific point in time (a year, date, or event) to show when a continuing action started. 'For' would be used with a duration (e.g., 'for 9 years').
Q3. Choose the synonym of 'Benevolent'.
A) Cruel
B) Kind ✓
C) Selfish
D) Arrogant
Explanation: Benevolent means well-meaning and charitable — closest in meaning to 'Kind'.
Q4. Choose the antonym of 'Scarce'.
A) Rare
B) Abundant ✓
C) Limited
D) Costly
Explanation: Scarce means in short supply. Its opposite is 'Abundant', meaning plentiful.
Q5. Identify the correct sentence.
A) He don't like tea.
B) He doesn't likes tea.
C) He doesn't like tea. ✓
D) He not like tea.
Explanation: With third-person singular subjects (he/she/it), the auxiliary 'doesn't' already carries the -s; the main verb stays in base form: 'doesn't like'.
Q6. One word for 'a person who loves books'.
A) Bibliophile ✓
B) Philanthropist
C) Linguist
D) Bibliographer
Explanation: A 'Bibliophile' is a lover of books. A Philanthropist is someone who donates to good causes; a Linguist studies languages; a Bibliographer compiles bibliographies.
Q7. Choose the correct passive voice: 'They are building a house.'
A) A house is built by them.
B) A house was being built by them.
C) A house is being built by them. ✓
D) A house has been built by them.
Explanation: The original sentence is Present Continuous ('are building'), so the passive must also be Present Continuous: 'is being built'.
Q8. Fill in the blank: Neither the manager nor the workers ___ satisfied.
A) is
B) was
C) are ✓
D) be
Explanation: In 'neither...nor' constructions, the verb agrees with the noun closer to it — here 'workers' (plural), so 'are' is correct.
Q9. Choose the correct indirect speech: He said, 'I am tired.'
A) He said that he is tired.
B) He said that he was tired. ✓
C) He says that he was tired.
D) He said that he will be tired.
Explanation: When the reporting verb ('said') is in the past tense, the tense inside the reported speech shifts back one step: 'am' → 'was'.
Q10. Choose the correctly punctuated sentence.
A) Its a nice day, isnt it?
B) It's a nice day, isn't it? ✓
C) Its' a nice day, isn't it
D) It is a nice day isnt it?
Explanation: 'It's' (contraction of 'it is') and 'isn't' (contraction of 'is not') both need apostrophes, and the sentence needs a closing question mark.
Q11. Choose the correct preposition: He is good ___ mathematics.
A) in
B) at ✓
C) on
D) with
Explanation: 'Good at' is the fixed idiomatic preposition used with skills or subjects, e.g., 'good at maths', 'good at sports'.
Q12. Choose the plural of 'Phenomenon'.
A) Phenomenons
B) Phenomena ✓
C) Phenomenas
D) Phenomenoms
Explanation: 'Phenomenon' is a Greek-origin word; its irregular plural is 'Phenomena', not the regular '-s' form.
Q11. Spot the error: "Neither of the students have submitted their assignment."
A) Neither
B) have ✓
C) their
D) No error
Explanation: "Neither" is singular and takes a singular verb — it should be "has submitted", not "have submitted".
Q12. Choose the correctly punctuated sentence.
A) She said, "I am ready".
B) She said, "I am ready." ✓
C) She said "I am ready."
D) She said, I am ready.
Explanation: In standard punctuation, the closing punctuation mark goes inside the quotation marks, and a comma introduces the quoted speech.
Q13. One-word substitution: "A person who can speak many languages."
A) Linguist
B) Polyglot ✓
C) Bilingual
D) Translator
Explanation: A "Polyglot" is someone who knows and can use several languages. A "Linguist" studies language academically, which is a different meaning.
Q14. Synonym of "Meticulous":
A) Careless
B) Precise ✓

Q15. Antonym of "Abundant":

Explanation: "Abundant" means present in great quantity; its opposite is "Scarce", meaning insufficient or rare.
Q16. Choose the correct idiom for "to reveal a secret":
A) Break the ice
B) Let the cat out of the bag ✓
C) Beat around the bush
D) Once in a blue moon
Explanation: "Let the cat out of the bag" means to accidentally reveal a secret. "Break the ice" means to ease tension; "Beat around the bush" means to avoid a topic.
Q17. Fill in the blank: "He has been working here ___ 2015."
A) for
B) since ✓
C) from
D) at
Explanation: "Since" is used with a specific starting point in time (2015); "for" is used with a duration (e.g., "for 9 years").
Q18. Choose the correctly spelled word.
A) Ocassion
B) Occasion ✓
C) Occassion
D) Ocasion
Explanation: The correct spelling is "Occasion" — double "c", single "s".
Q19. Identify the correctly formed passive voice: "The manager will complete the report."
A) The report is completed by the manager.
B) The report will be completed by the manager. ✓
C) The report was completed by the manager.
D) The report has been completed by the manager.
Explanation: Future simple active ("will complete") becomes future simple passive: "will be + past participle" — "will be completed".
Q20. Reading comprehension: "Despite the heavy rain, the match continued because the ground had good drainage." Why did the match continue?
A) The rain stopped suddenly
B) The players insisted
C) The ground drained water well ✓
D) The umpire ignored the rain
Explanation: The sentence directly states the ground "had good drainage", which is why the match wasn't interrupted despite heavy rain.
Q21. Choose the correct article: "___ European Union has 27 member states."
A) A
B) The ✓
C) An
D) No article
Explanation: Although "European" starts with a vowel letter, it starts with a consonant sound ("yoo-ropean"), so "a/an" rules don't apply here — "The" is used because it's a specific, unique organisation.
Q22. Choose the word that means the opposite of "Reluctant":
A) Hesitant
B) Willing ✓
C) Unwilling
D) Doubtful
Explanation: "Reluctant" means unwilling or hesitant; its opposite is "Willing".
Q23. Spot the error: "Each of the boys have brought their own lunch."
A) Each
B) have ✓
C) brought
D) No error
Explanation: "Each" is singular, so the correct verb is "has brought", not "have brought".
Q24. One-word substitution: "One who does not believe in the existence of God."
A) Agnostic
B) Atheist ✓
C) Theist
D) Pessimist
Explanation: An "Atheist" denies the existence of God, while an "Agnostic" believes it cannot be known for certain either way.
Q25. Choose the correctly ordered sentence: "always / She / on time / arrives."
A) Always she arrives on time.
B) She always arrives on time. ✓
C) She arrives always on time.
